# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Casual Spotify listeners who have accumulated a large "liked songs" library and feel it has become unnavigable. They don't want to manually curate playlists. They want the tool to understand their taste and do the work quietly. They open Sortify when they want to see what's been sorted, check what needs a decision, or trust that their library is in order. Ambient use — not a daily active workflow app.

## Product Purpose

Sortify automatically classifies Spotify liked songs into user-defined playlists using a 3-level engine (rules, clustering, LLM). Success looks like: the user's full liked-songs library is organized, coherent playlists reflect their actual taste, and the inbox is near-zero with minimal effort. The user shouldn't have to think about the system — it should just work.

## Brand Personality

Smart, Warm, Precise. The voice is a knowledgeable friend who understands music and quietly keeps your library in order. It surfaces insight without showing off. It never explains what it's doing in technical terms.

## Anti-references

- Spotify's own UI — too familiar, would feel like a skin of the original rather than a distinct tool
- Generic SaaS dashboard — Stripe-style metric grids, cold neutrals, enterprise feel
- Music streaming dark mode cliché — neon accents on pure black, heavy gradients, feels like a streaming app clone
- Overly minimal / boring — plain grey or white with no personality, feels unfinished or unintentional

## Design Principles

1. **Understands without explaining** — Show what was classified, where, and how coherent it is. Don't expose the engine internals unless the user asks.
2. **Warmth through music texture** — Use color and atmosphere that feels adjacent to how music feels (mood, genre, energy) — not generic app chrome.
3. **Precision at every scale** — Every number (track count, coherence score, valence) should feel intentional and readable. No decorative data.
4. **Calm confidence** — No states that apologize. Empty inbox means success. Zero items is a win, not a problem to solve.
5. **Personal, not generic** — The experience should feel tuned to this user's library specifically. Avoid patterns that look like any Spotify-adjacent tool could use them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Basic usability. Good contrast, readable text sizes, no color-only communication for critical states. No hard WCAG level requirement.
